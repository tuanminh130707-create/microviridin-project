# Validating a Homolog-Set Curation Strategy for ConSurf Analysis of the Microviridin Macrocyclase MdnC

## TL;DR
- **Your instinct is correct and is directly supported by the primary literature: the rigorous way to define your conservation set is a monophyletic microviridin clade drawn from a phylogenetic tree, and taxonomic restriction to cyanobacteria alone is necessary but NOT sufficient.** HMM gathering-threshold classification and taxonomic filtering are legitimate first passes, but they cannot separate genuine microviridin MdnC from the many ester-forming graspetide ligases that share the ATP-grasp fold — you must use the IQ-TREE clade (ideally cross-checked with an EFI-EST sequence-similarity network) as the actual set-definition step.
- **Your graded result (Asp192 invariant, Asn195 variable, Glu191 only "acidic") is a real, publishable biological signal, not a small-sample artifact — and it is explicitly corroborated by the primary structural paper.** Li et al. (2016) state verbatim that "In most other MdnC-like macrocyclases, Glu191 is replaced by an aspartate residue, while Asp192 is well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases," matching your finding exactly. This is the signature of a specificity-determining position (Asp192) versus a physicochemically-constrained position (Glu191).
- **For reliability, aim for a curated set of roughly 50–150 (up to 300) non-redundant cyanobacterial microviridin MdnC sequences, cluster at 90–95% (never below 80%), and use ConSurf's empirical-Bayesian method with its confidence-interval flag.** At that sample size an invariant Asp192 will robustly earn grade 9, and ConSurf's own "insufficient data" rule (≥4-grade confidence interval or <6 non-gapped residues) is the built-in guard that tells you whether the Asn195/Glu191 grades are trustworthy.

## Key Findings

**1. The "shared fold, divergent function" problem is exactly what ConSurf warns about, and it has a name.** ConSurf's own documentation distinguishes two use modes: collecting *as many homologues as possible* to find residues important for the overall fold/superfamily, versus a *smaller alignment of only close homologues that share the function* to find residues specific to a family/sub-family. Your first failed run (28–29% identity distant ATP-grasp ligases) was the former; your project needs the latter. The literature term for positions like Asp192 that are conserved within one functional subfamily but differ between subfamilies is **specificity-determining positions (SDPs)**. Contaminating your set with non-microviridin graspetide ligases converts Asp192 from a within-set invariant (high grade) into a between-subfamily SDP (flattened grade) — precisely the "flattened signal" you observed.

**2. ConSurf's documented numeric recommendations.** Minimum 5 sequences to run at all (ConSurf-DB performs "no calculation … due to insufficient data" below 5); a rule-of-thumb minimum — "we recommend a minimum of 10 homologues for any ConSurf run"; recommended working range — "We recommend including between 50-and-300 homologs." ConSurf-DB uses "A maximum of 300 sequences … (the 300 with the lowest expectation values)," and its high-accuracy profiles mostly draw on 201–300 sequences. CD-HIT redundancy clustering should be set **between 80% and 95%** — Rubin (2021, *Current Protocols*) states "the sequence identity cut-off should be between 95% and 80%. Using a higher threshold may compromise the integrity of the final ConSurf analysis, as the relevant sequences may lack diversity." Default minimum identity to the query is 35% (upper bound of the "twilight zone"). The modern default "increased the default number of putative homologous sequences to 150, but reduced the HMMER E-value cutoff to 0.0001 to minimize the risk of including non-homologues." Default MSA algorithm is MAFFT; default rate method is empirical Bayesian.

**3. Taxonomic restriction vs. monophyletic clade vs. SSN — and why you likely need all three in sequence.** Taxonomic restriction (cyanobacteria only) is a coarse but necessary filter — and one you have not applied yet. What your data *have* shown is that the **HMM/chemistry threshold alone** is insufficient: ~58% of your HMM-passing pool is non-cyanobacterial, which is precisely the fraction a cyanobacteria-only filter would remove (so the 58% argues *for* taxonomic restriction, not against it). What taxonomic restriction cannot do *on its own* is confirm that every remaining cyanobacterial ester-former is genuinely microviridin MdnC — a few cyanobacterial ATP-grasp enzymes are paralogs or other graspetides — and that residual, not-yet-demonstrated risk is why selecting a **monophyletic clade** from a query-containing tree is the gold-standard functional-set definition when a fold spans multiple functions, and there is direct precedent in this exact family: Makarova et al. (2022) and Ramesh et al. (2021) both used ATP-grasp phylogenies to separate microviridin-type ligases from other graspetide and RimK-like ligases. **EFI-EST sequence-similarity networks (SSNs)** are the complementary genomic-enzymology standard for carving a functionally heterogeneous superfamily into "isofunctional clusters"; the EFI team explicitly notes that the alignment-score threshold separating functions "must be determined empirically." Best practice: taxonomic filter → tree/SSN to define the microviridin clade → ConSurf on that clade.

**4. Direct primary-literature grounding for your graded result.** Li, Condurso, Li, Ding & Bruner (2016, *Nature Chemical Biology*) mutated the α7 residues and found the charge-reversal double mutant MdnC(E191K/D192K) completely abolished MdnA binding and macrocyclization, while the milder MdnC(E191A/D192A) retained partial activity. They report that "MdnA1–35 interacts with the central domain of MdnC through electrostatic interactions between Arg17 of MdnA and Glu191/Asp192/Asn195 of MdnC α7," and — crucially for your interpretation — that "In most other MdnC-like macrocyclases, Glu191 is replaced by an aspartate residue, while Asp192 is well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases." This is the published statement of your exact graded pattern.

**5. Precedent for conservation/ConSurf analysis in RiPP and ATP-grasp enzymes, and the contamination lesson.** The graspetide field has repeatedly documented that ATP-grasp ligases are, per Makarova et al. (2022), "difficult to distinguish, without phylogenetic analysis, from other families of ATP-grasp enzymes … including the ligases that catalyze N-terminal to C-terminal cyclization in head-to-tail bacterial peptides and the α-guanidino amino acid-peptide ligase …, and from the RimK enzymes that catalyze the maturation of the ribosomal protein S6." Genome-mining pipelines (RODEO, EFI-EST SSNs) are the standard remedy. This is the documented, field-specific version of the subfamily-contamination confound you hit.

**6. The statistics: your pattern is meaningful, with caveats built into ConSurf.** ConSurf/Rate4Site uses an empirical-Bayesian (EB) rate estimator that Mayrose et al. (2004) showed is far more accurate than maximum likelihood at small N (mean-square error 0.51 vs 2.92 at six sequences), converging as N grows. Reliability is governed by a **50% confidence interval** (25th–75th percentiles of the inferred rate distribution); a position is flagged "insufficient data" (colored light yellow) if it has **<6 non-gapped residues OR its confidence interval spans ≥4 color grades**. A residue invariant across ~30–60 non-gapped sequences robustly earns grade 9; a position with one or two substitutions is graded lower but still reliably if the CI is narrow.

## Details

### Section 1 — Best practices for constructing a "functionally equivalent" homolog set

The foundational assumption of ConSurf (and all Rate4Site-based conservation scoring) is that functionally important residues evolve slowly under purifying selection. This assumption only holds *within a set of sequences that actually share the function of interest*. ConSurf's FAQ states the design choice plainly: for "a functional site that is specific for a certain family/sub-family, the alignment should be smaller and include only close homologues that share the function," and adds the key caveat — "in reality, we might not know which of the proteins share the same function." Your project is unusually strong precisely because you *do* have external evidence (HMM class, taxonomy, tree position, and a small alignment showing divergence at 191/192/195) to define function.

Two homolog categories are relevant. **Orthologs** (same protein across species, same function) are, per ConSurf, "used for analysis of specific functions that are shared by a protein family/sub-family." **Paralogs** (duplicated within a genome, function may have drifted) "should be used to explore the major structural and functional characteristics of the protein family/superfamily." The distant 28–29%-identity ATP-grasp ligases that contaminated your first run behave as paralogs/out-group: they share the fold but not the microviridin leader-recognition function, so including them tells ConSurf that positions 191/192/195 are "variable" when in fact they are invariant *within microviridin MdnC*.

The formal concept here is **functional divergence** and **specificity-determining positions (SDPs)** — positions conserved within a subfamily but conserved-to-a-different-residue across subfamilies. The SDP literature (Teppa et al. 2012; Rausell & Valencia, *PNAS* 2010) shows these positions preferentially cluster at binding interfaces — exactly where your α7 leader-recognition residues sit. The practical lesson: contamination by a sister subfamily does not just add noise, it *systematically* converts your most informative SDP (Asp192) into an apparently variable position, which is the mechanistic explanation for why "clearing the HMM ester-former threshold was not sufficient."

### Section 2 — ConSurf's own recommendations on set composition

Consolidated from the ConSurf server documentation, ConSurf 2016 (Ashkenazy et al., *NAR* 44:W344), ConSurf-DB (Ben Chorin et al. 2020, *Protein Science*), and Rubin (2021, *Current Protocols*):

- **Minimum sequences:** 5 required; ~10 recommended as a floor ("we recommend a minimum of 10 homologues for any ConSurf run"); below this, EB is essential.
- **Recommended number:** "between 50-and-300 homologs." ConSurf-DB caps at "A maximum of 300 sequences," and its high-accuracy pre-calculated profiles mostly use 201–300. The modern default collects up to 150 with an E-value cutoff of 0.0001.
- **Redundancy (CD-HIT upper bound):** cluster at 80–95%; default 95%. Do NOT cluster lower than 80% — you would strip diversity. ConSurf-DB discards sequences >95% identical to the query to reduce sampling bias.
- **Minimum identity (lower bound):** default 35% (twilight-zone edge). For a within-subfamily analysis you will naturally be well above this — your cyanobacterial microviridin set should be far more similar than 35%.
- **MSA:** MAFFT default (PRANK, T-COFFEE, MUSCLE, CLUSTALW also available).
- **Rate method:** empirical Bayesian (default) over ML.
- **Phylogenetic breadth changes grades:** ConSurf's benchmark shows Rate4Site's Spearman correlation to true rates rising monotonically with taxon count (≈0.831 at 16 sequences → 0.944 at 128 → 0.974 at 512). More importantly, the *composition* matters: because Rate4Site weights substitutions by branch length, a set dominated by near-identical sequences yields little signal, while a set spanning appropriate cyanobacterial diversity maximizes discrimination. Redundancy is downweighted automatically by the tree — one of ConSurf's core strengths over entropy/consensus methods — so prioritize genuine taxonomic breadth *within* the microviridin clade rather than raw sequence count.

**Explicit guidance on broad vs. narrow:** Yes — the ConSurf FAQ gives it directly. Broad/superfamily set → fold-level residues; narrow/subfamily set of shared-function close homologs → function-specific residues. Your goal (leader-recognition residues specific to microviridin) mandates the narrow set.

### Section 3 — Taxonomic restriction vs. monophyletic clade vs. SSN

| Approach | What it does | Strengths | Weaknesses | Precedent in this family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Taxonomic filter (cyanobacteria only)** | Removes non-cyanobacterial sequences (~58% of your HMM-passing pool) | Fast, biologically motivated (microviridin is cyanobacterial) | Coarse, not a functional test — can't confirm each *remaining* cyanobacterial ester-former is genuinely microviridin MdnC (some cyanobacterial ATP-grasp enzymes are paralogs or other graspetides) | Microviridin universally described as cyanobacterial |
| **Monophyletic clade from tree (IQ-TREE)** | Selects the branch containing the query MdnC | Gold standard when a fold spans functions; directly identifies the functionally-equivalent group; gives branch support | Requires good alignment + model; clade boundary can be a judgment call at weakly-supported nodes | Makarova 2022; Ramesh 2021; Lee 2020 all used ATP-grasp phylogeny to delimit graspetide subgroups |
| **SSN via EFI-EST** | All-by-all BLAST; clusters at an empirically-chosen alignment-score threshold | Standard genomic-enzymology tool for isofunctional clusters; scales to thousands; visual | Threshold must be tuned empirically; not model-based | Ramesh 2021 and Lee 2020 both built ATP-grasp SSNs to define the graspetide groups (12 → 24) |

**Recommendation:** combine them in sequence. Taxonomic filter first (cheap), then define the set by the **monophyletic microviridin clade** in your IQ-TREE tree, and *cross-validate* that clade against an EFI-EST SSN cluster if feasible. Convergence of tree-clade and SSN-cluster membership is strong evidence you have a clean isofunctional set. The EFI team's own caution — that the score separating functions "must be determined empirically" because "the dependence of functional divergence on sequence divergence is not uniform across protein families" — is exactly why you should not rely on a single identity cutoff. Note the conceptual difference: a phylogenetic tree explicitly models the evolutionary process (and is a prerequisite for ConSurf's probabilistic rate estimation), whereas an SSN is a fast similarity graph; using both guards against the weaknesses of each.

### Section 4 — MdnC/MdnB functional literature and the α7 residues

Li, Condurso, Li, Ding & Bruner (2016, *Nat. Chem. Biol.*; PDB 5IG8 apo-MdnB, 5IG9 MdnC·MdnA leader complex) established the mechanism. Key points for your interpretation:

- MdnC (ester/lactone former; homolog of MvdD) and MdnB (amide/lactam former; homolog of MvdC) are three-domain ATP-grasp proteins related to RimK. The ester former installs the two lactones; the amide former then installs the lactam.
- The MdnA leader peptide's conserved **PFFARFL** motif folds as an α-helix and binds MdnC; leader binding orients helix α7 and drives a large (~25 Å) shift of the β9β10 hairpin that opens the core-binding site — an allosteric activation.
- "MdnA1–35 interacts with the central domain of MdnC through electrostatic interactions between Arg17 of MdnA and Glu191/Asp192/Asn195 of MdnC α7." Removing the α-helical leader element or the "ED" residues abolishes cyclization; the N-terminus of the substrate is dispensable, and the leader α-helix can activate MdnC *in trans*.
- **Mutagenesis:** "The charge reversal of the conserved Glu191 and Asp192 residues completely abolished the MdnA/MdnCKK interaction … and MdnCKK had no macrocyclization activity toward the full length MdnA. On the other hand, the more conservative mutant MdnCAA [E191A/D192A] retained partial cyclization activity but had no detectable MdnA/MdnCAA binding interaction via ITC." This establishes Glu191/Asp192 as functionally critical for leader recognition.
- **The conservation statement you need (verbatim):** "In most other MdnC-like macrocyclases, Glu191 is replaced by an aspartate residue, while Asp192 is well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases."

This gives a direct, citable grounding for a graded ConSurf result:
- **Asp192 invariant (expected grade 9):** matches "well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases" — a specificity-determining, functionally essential contact.
- **Glu191 conserved only as acidic character:** matches "replaced by an aspartate residue" in most homologs — the *negative charge* is conserved (needed to contact Arg17) but the exact identity Glu vs Asp is not. Because ConSurf scores identity, this position should score intermediate; interpret it via physicochemical conservation, not raw grade.
- **Asn195 mostly N but His in deep-branching lineages (Cylindrospermum, Hapalosiphonaceae):** consistent with a peripheral/secondary contact that tolerates substitution; the N→H change in early-diverging clades is itself phylogenetically informative and should be reported, not hidden.

Downstream work reinforces the theme. The PsnB plesiocin study (Song et al. 2021, *Nat. Chem. Biol.*) shows the analogous leader-recognition machinery and a highly conserved active-site residue that recognizes a ring-forming acidic residue — evidence that leader/precursor-recognition residues are subfamily-specific across graspetide groups. To my finding, **no published paper has performed a dedicated ConSurf/Rate4Site conservation analysis of MdnC α7 across a curated microviridin clade** — which is what makes your project a genuine (if modest) contribution rather than a replication.

### Section 5 — Precedent and the contamination lesson in RiPP/ATP-grasp families

- **SSN + RODEO is the field standard** for parsing RiPP biosynthetic enzymes into functional groups (lasso peptides, lanthipeptides, graspetides, etc.). Ramesh et al. (2021, *ACS Chem. Biol.*) used a RODEO update to identify thousands of high-confidence graspetide BGCs and, with sequence/co-occurrence and SSN analysis, doubled the number of graspetide groups from 12 to 24 — an explicit demonstration that these ligases only resolve into functional groups after network/phylogenetic partitioning.
- **The specific confound is documented:** Makarova et al. (2022, *Biol. Direct*) state graspetide ATP-grasp ligases are "difficult to distinguish, without phylogenetic analysis, from other families of ATP-grasp enzymes … including the ligases that catalyze N-terminal to C-terminal cyclization in head-to-tail bacterial peptides and the α-guanidino amino acid-peptide ligase …, and from the RimK enzymes that catalyze the maturation of the ribosomal protein S6."
- **Cross-phylum spread is real and is your contamination source:** microviridin-type core sequences and ester-forming graspetide ligases occur across Cyanobacteria, Proteobacteria (Pseudomonadota), Bacteroidetes, and Acidobacteria (Zhao et al. 2021, *JACS*; Ramesh 2021; Makarova 2022), "a much broader distribution than previously realized." These Bacteroidetes/Proteobacteria ligases are *bona fide* ester-forming graspetide macrocyclases — they will pass an ester-former HMM threshold — but they are not microviridin and, as you observed, diverge at 191/192/195. This is the mechanistic reason your HMM+threshold step alone could not clean the set: TIGRFAM models classify by family/fold, not by leader-recognition specificity.

### Section 6 — Statistical reliability of your graded pattern

**Is one-residue-invariant / adjacent-residue-variable a real signal or small-N artifact?** With a properly curated set it is a real signal, and it is the textbook signature of differential selective constraint on adjacent positions — common at binding interfaces (Section 1's SDP literature). The determining factor is not the raw count but ConSurf's confidence interval:

- Rate4Site EB assigns each position a **50% confidence interval** — "based on a confidence interval that is defined by the lower and upper quartiles: the 25th and 75th percentiles of the inferred distribution of conservation scores" (Landau et al. 2005, citing Susko et al. 2002, *Mol. Biol. Evol.* 19:1514). "When the number of sequences is small, the confidence interval tends to be large, meaning a low level of support to the inferred conservation score."
- A position is flagged **"insufficient data" (light yellow)** if it has **fewer than 6 non-gapped residues** or its **CI spans ≥4 color grades**: "Conservation scores that are obtained for positions in the alignment that have less than 6 un-gapped amino acids are considered to be unreliable … If the interval in a specific position spans 4 or more color grades the score is considered as unreliable." This is your built-in artifact detector — run ConSurf, then check whether Asn195 and Glu191 are yellow-flagged. If they are *not* flagged, their intermediate grades are trustworthy and the graded pattern is real.
- Mayrose et al. (2004) quantified EB's small-N advantage: "The MSE between the inferred rates and their simulated values was below 0.52 for as few as six sequences. In contrast, a comparable level of accuracy is not achievable with ML even when the number of sequences is as large as 30," and specifically "MSE = 2.92 and 2.0 for six and 12 sequences, respectively, compared with 0.51 and 0.32, respectively, for EB-EXP."
- **Column occupancy matters (the definitive worked example):** in Bcl-xL, Mayrose 2004 report that Arg6 and Arg139 are both fully conserved, but "while Arg139 is present in all 32 homologs, Arg6 appears in only 11 of them. ML could not discriminate between these two positions and assigned both the highest conservation score. In contrast, EB-EXP rated Arg139 as the most conserved position." Lesson: ensure your MSA has good, low-gap coverage at columns 191/192/195, which a within-clade alignment should provide.
- **One documented caveat:** REL/empirical-Bayesian estimators like Rate4Site "tend to overestimate the rates of sites with no or very few mutations" — i.e., they are conservative at nearly-invariant sites. If anything this makes ConSurf *under*-call the conservation of your invariant Asp192, strengthening rather than weakening a grade-9 result.

**Practical sample-size target:** your 6-sequence preliminary check is enough to *see* the pattern but sits at ConSurf's reliability floor for the varying positions. Scale to ~50–150 non-redundant cyanobacterial microviridin MdnC sequences. At that N, an invariant Asp192 will earn a narrow-CI grade 9, and the Asn195 N→H variation will be estimated with a trustworthy (non-flagged) intermediate grade — making your graded interpretation defensible.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Finalize the set definition (do this before any ConSurf run).**
1. Keep your HMM/TIGRFAM classification (TIGR04184/MvdD ester-formers vs TIGR04185/MvdC amide-formers) and gathering-threshold cutoffs as the *first* filter, and the cyanobacteria-only taxonomic filter as the *second* — but treat neither as the set definition.
2. Build the phylogenetic tree with **IQ-TREE** (a maximum-likelihood tree-building program). Turn on **ModelFinder** with the `-m MFP` flag — ModelFinder automatically tests many models of how amino acids substitute over evolutionary time and picks the best-fitting one (e.g. LG+G4), which you then carry into ConSurf as its substitution model. Add two *independent* branch-support measures, each run with ≥1000 replicates: **ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot)** — a fast resampling test that estimates how reproducible each branch is, reported 0–100 — and **SH-aLRT** (the Shimodaira–Hasegawa approximate likelihood-ratio test, a second, differently-derived branch-support score, also 0–100). Then define your conservation set as the **monophyletic clade** — a group on the tree consisting of one ancestor and *all* of its descendants, i.e. the natural "these sequences share a common origin (and here, a function)" unit — that contains the 5IG9 MdnC sequence. Draw the clade boundary only at *well-supported* branches (a rule of thumb: trustworthy when UFBoot ≥ 95 **and** SH-aLRT ≥ 80). Include the Bacteroidetes/Proteobacteria/Acidobacteria ester-formers in the tree as an **out-group** (sequences deliberately placed outside your clade of interest, used to root the tree and make the clade's boundary visible), then exclude that out-group from the ConSurf set.
3. If feasible, generate an EFI-EST SSN of the same ligase pool and confirm your tree-clade coincides with a single isofunctional SSN cluster at a tuned alignment-score threshold. Convergence of the two methods is your validation.

**Stage 2 — Configure ConSurf.**
4. Target 50–150 non-redundant sequences (ConSurf-DB caps at 300); CD-HIT cluster at 90–95% (never below 80%); MAFFT alignment; empirical-Bayesian rate method.
5. Provide your curated clade as a pre-built external MSA (ConSurf accepts external MSAs) rather than letting ConSurf's default HMMER search re-contaminate the set with distant ATP-grasp hits.
6. Map grades onto PDB 5IG9 and specifically inspect the confidence-interval / "insufficient data" flags at residues 191, 192, 195.

**Stage 3 — Interpret.**
7. Report Asp192 as an invariant, high-confidence (grade 9) specificity-determining residue, citing Li 2016's "well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases" and the E191K/D192K abolition of binding and activity.
8. Report Glu191 as a position under *physicochemical* (acidic-character) rather than *identity* conservation — cite Li 2016's "Glu191 is replaced by an aspartate residue" statement — and note this is why its raw ConSurf grade is intermediate; consider a supplementary physicochemical-conservation view (e.g., grouping D/E).
9. Report Asn195's N→H substitution in deep-branching lineages (Cylindrospermum, Hapalosiphonaceae) as a phylogenetically-structured, lower-constraint position; verify it is not "insufficient data"-flagged before drawing conclusions.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that would change these recommendations:**
- If your clean cyanobacterial clade yields **<~25–30 sequences**, keep CD-HIT high (don't over-cluster) and lean on the EB confidence intervals; report Asn195/Glu191 as "suggestive, CI-limited." If it yields **<10**, treat all non-invariant grades as tentative.
- If the microviridin clade is **not monophyletic / poorly supported** in IQ-TREE, fall back to the SSN cluster as the primary set definition and report the tree ambiguity.
- If positions 191/192/195 come back **yellow ("insufficient data")** despite N>50, check alignment gap content at those columns — gappy columns, not true variability, are the usual cause (the Arg6 lesson).
- Run a small **sensitivity analysis**: confirm Asp192's grade 9 is robust to two or three reasonable alternative clade boundaries; robustness there is your strongest defense against the "artifact" critique.

## Caveats
- **No published ConSurf/Rate4Site analysis of MdnC α7 exists** to my finding, so you have no direct methodological precedent for *this exact residue set*; your validation rests on transferring general ConSurf best practice plus the Li 2016 conservation statements. Frame the work as consistent with, not a replication of, prior results.
- **Li 2016's "well conserved among all microviridin macrocyclases" is a qualitative statement referencing a supplementary alignment**, not a quantitative conservation score; your ConSurf run operationalizes it. Describe your result as quantifying an observation the primary authors made qualitatively.
- **REL/empirical-Bayesian estimators (Rate4Site) compress the low end of the rate range and can slightly overestimate rates at nearly-invariant sites** — this makes ConSurf conservative for your invariant Asp192, which strengthens a grade-9 result.
- **Clade-boundary choice is a judgment call.** Where you cut the tree changes the set; always report the bootstrap/SH-aLRT support at the boundary node.
- **HMM gathering thresholds classify by fold/family, not by leader-recognition function** — that is structurally why they could not exclude the Bacteroidetes ester-formers; do not expect any HMM-only refinement to fix the contamination.
- **Some cited items are reviews or preprints**; I prioritized the peer-reviewed primary versions where both exist. Where a claim rests on a review, treat it as a pointer to the underlying primary paper.

## Glossary
- **ATP-grasp ligase:** enzyme family that uses ATP to activate a carboxylate (as an acyl-phosphate) for bond formation; in microviridin, forms the ester/amide macrocycle crosslinks. Defined by a shared "grasp" fold that spans many unrelated functions.
- **ConSurf:** web server that estimates per-residue evolutionary conservation from a multiple sequence alignment + phylogenetic tree and maps a 1–9 grade (1 = variable, 9 = conserved) onto a structure.
- **Rate4Site:** the underlying algorithm ConSurf uses; computes a site-specific evolutionary rate using the phylogenetic tree and a probabilistic substitution model.
- **Empirical Bayesian (EB) rate estimation:** a method that combines observed data with a prior rate distribution; more accurate than maximum likelihood when few sequences are available, and provides confidence intervals.
- **Confidence interval / "insufficient data":** ConSurf's 50% CI (25th–75th percentile of the inferred rate distribution); a position is flagged unreliable (light yellow) if it has <6 non-gapped residues or its CI spans ≥4 grades.
- **Graspetide (a.k.a. ω-ester-containing peptide, OEP):** RiPP class defined by side-chain-to-side-chain ester/amide macrocycles installed by ATP-grasp ligases; microviridins are the founding subgroup.
- **RiPP:** ribosomally synthesized and post-translationally modified peptide; a precursor peptide (leader + core) is enzymatically modified, then the leader is cleaved.
- **Leader / core / precursor peptide:** the precursor (MdnA) has an N-terminal leader (recognized by the enzyme; contains the PFFARFL α-helix motif) and a C-terminal core (chemically modified into the macrocycle).
- **Ortholog / paralog:** orthologs are the same gene across species (conserve function, use for subfamily-specific analysis); paralogs are duplicates within a genome (function can diverge, use for superfamily-level analysis).
- **Specificity-determining position (SDP):** a residue conserved within a functional subfamily but conserved-to-a-different-residue between subfamilies; typically at binding interfaces and diagnostic of functional divergence — the category Asp192 falls into.
- **Functional divergence:** the process by which duplicated genes acquire different functions, producing subfamily-specific conservation patterns.
- **Monophyletic clade:** a group on a phylogenetic tree consisting of an ancestor and all its descendants — the natural unit for defining a functionally-equivalent set.
- **Out-group:** sequences deliberately placed outside the clade of interest; used to root a phylogenetic tree and reveal where the clade of interest begins and ends.
- **IQ-TREE:** a maximum-likelihood program for building phylogenetic trees, with built-in model selection (ModelFinder) and branch-support tests (UFBoot, SH-aLRT).
- **ModelFinder (`-m MFP`):** IQ-TREE's model-selection step; `MFP` = "ModelFinder Plus." It tests many amino-acid substitution models and picks the best-fitting one (e.g. LG+G4), which then feeds both the tree and ConSurf.
- **Ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot):** a fast resampling method (run with `-B`, e.g. `-B 1000`) that estimates how reproducible each branch of the tree is; reported 0–100, with ≥95 conventionally "well supported."
- **SH-aLRT (Shimodaira–Hasegawa approximate likelihood-ratio test):** a second, independent branch-support test (run with `-alrt`, e.g. `-alrt 1000`); reported 0–100, with ≥80 conventionally "well supported." Using it alongside UFBoot makes a branch's support more trustworthy when both pass.
- **Sequence-similarity network (SSN):** a graph where nodes are sequences and edges connect pairs above a chosen similarity threshold; used (EFI-EST) to split a superfamily into isofunctional clusters.
- **Isofunctional cluster:** an SSN cluster whose members are inferred to share the same enzymatic function; the threshold that produces it must be found empirically.
- **HMM / gathering threshold:** a hidden Markov model profile (e.g., TIGRFAM/NCBIFAM) scores whether a sequence belongs to a family; the "gathering" (trusted) cutoff is the curated bit-score above which membership is asserted. Classifies by family, not by fine-grained substrate/leader specificity.
- **Twilight zone:** the ~20–35% sequence-identity range where homology becomes hard to assert reliably; ConSurf's default minimum-identity floor is 35%.
- **CD-HIT:** a fast sequence-clustering tool ConSurf uses to remove redundant near-identical sequences at a user-set identity threshold (recommended 80–95%).
